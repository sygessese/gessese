---
title: "The grief of Arrival"
date: "2025-07-26"
description: "People often speak of the beauty of joy within suffering."
---

People often speak of the beauty of joy within suffering. The resilience of those who find slivers of light in the darkness, who cultivate a kind of joy that doesn’t erase pain but insists on blooming beside it. It’s beautiful, yes. It’s humbling. There’s nobility in it — an almost sacred defiance. But what’s less spoken about, rarely acknowledged, is what comes after for those that forge a path out. What it feels like to survive something, and then to arrive somewhere entirely different. Somewhere soft. Safe. Abundant. And to suddenly see, with shocking clarity, the full weight of what had been endured.

The grief doesn’t surface in the midst of suffering. It waits. It lingers in the body, hiding in crevices. And it assumes form only in contrast, when held up beside the lives of those who were never touched by that kind of ache. It emerges slowly, not just from memory, but from observation. From sitting across a table and watching how some people’s lives were incubated within a plush gentleness. How they were allowed to just be, held, delighted in. How their emotions were met with care, not silence. Homes filled with laughter, community, warmth — not the aching quiet of isolation.

There’s a devastating kind of heartbreak that comes from realizing the depth of one’s own starvation only when witnessing others who were fed all along. The grief is not envy. It’s a paralyzing disbelief. The helpless ache of why. A disorienting mourning of the injustice in it all.

What once felt normal was, in fact, an absence. That survival required a kind of self-erasure. Of hope, of desire. It asked of you to turn your chin up and declare that nothing was in fact missing. The contrast, when faced with what actually was missing, can be disorienting. Not just because others lived differently, but because they lived that way all along. While one was learning how to bury longing, another was learning how to belong. While one curled up in bed to escape the emptiness, another was outside, throwing their head back in laughing, basking in the sun, feeling held by life. And that realization can split the heart open in new ways.

This is the grief of arrival. And it’s an isolating grief, because neither those you left behind, nor those where you’ve arrived, can understand the terrain you’ve crossed to get here.
